# ch23-what-survives

**Objective.** A reader of chapter 23 who meets the objection "too much process" learns that what survives of the method in every project is the kit's six rules, each answering a failure chapter 6 names, and that a check is each project's own, let in by an error that project met, instead of reading Ninjobs' two privacy checks as part of the method, in both editions. Found by `m15-review` (work/done/m15-review.md, B4: "23:23: Ninjobs' two product checks given as what survives of the method. Seen.").

**Decided without the author.** The batch had no one to answer; each choice is the recommended one.

1. The six rules stay, named as the kit's: the kit's `SETUP.md` §2 lists six as "the rules the four commands keep, in every project" (read in the kit's repository), and chapter 16's gain already calls them "the ones the kit's `SETUP.md` lists for every project". So they are the method's, and only the two checks are Ninjobs'. Chapter 6 §Each rule and its failure is where the book teaches six rules of the kit, each with its failure, so the pointer goes there (decision 4). The two lists do not match one for one (`SETUP.md` has the docs/05 slots and living documents where chapter 6 has commands that hold no fact and the queue); that is not this line's to settle, and the new sentence holds either way. Source: `SETUP.md` §2; chapter 6 §Each rule and its failure; chapter 16 §What the team gains; the queue line.
2. The two checks leave the sentence, and no count of checks replaces them: the method fixes no number of checks; each project lets one in when it names an error that project met. That is chapter 16's own wording ("the governor decides there, with an error that project met", §The error that earned a mark), so the new clause adds no claim chapter 16 does not already prove. Source: chapter 16 §The error that earned a mark; docs/03 `governor`.
3. Ninjobs' privacy checks are not retold in chapter 23, not even as a parenthesis: chapter 16 tells them, and the sentence before already links chapter 16; a retelling would put the story in two chapters. Source: docs/04 **Numbers that prove** and **Chapter pointers**; chapter 16 §It works both ways.
4. A pointer back to chapter 6 is added, inside the sentence, as `[chapter 6](06-birth-of-focus-kit.md)` / `[capítulo 6](06-birth-of-focus-kit.md)`: "six rules" is a count the reader of an adoption chapter (often a manager who skipped ahead, docs/00 §Audience) cannot use without the list, and chapter 6 holds the list with each rule's failure. docs/04 **Chapter pointers** keeps a pointer back that leans on a specific detail worth reopening; this is one, and chapter 23 has no other link to chapter 6. The six rules are not listed inline: a list would retell chapter 6 inside an answer of three sentences. Source: docs/04 **Chapter pointers** and **Teach, do not only show**.
5. The Portuguese edition says "verificação" for a check of the process, the word chapters 3, 6 and 16 use in Portuguese for it ("29 verificações", "duas verificações"); "checagem" is kept in the edition for a type check (chapter 3's exhaustive check), so "checagens" leaves chapter 23. Source: docs/03 (no `check` row); pt chapters 3, 6 and 16; `pt-fixed-terms` in docs/06.
6. The sentence after it ("A team that finds a step that catches nothing removes it, and the method expects it to.") stays: it says what the team does, and the new sentence says what the method keeps; they do not repeat each other. The gain and key points are unchanged: neither mentions the rules or the checks. Source: chapter 23 §What the team gains and §Key points; docs/04 **One gain**.
7. No docs/03 term, no new queue line, no split: one sentence per edition. Source: docs/03 (`governor` exists; "rule" and "check" are plain words); docs/05 §1.
