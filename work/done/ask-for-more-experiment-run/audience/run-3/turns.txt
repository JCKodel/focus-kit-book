# Wrenmere Lending Library: 2025 annual report, board summary

*For the Board meeting of 19 February 2026. The report notes that all its figures are synthetic.*

## Bottom line

2025 was a mixed year. Membership hit a record and children's borrowing grew strongly, but total loans fell for the first time in four years. Most of the fall came from three events that are now over or being fixed. The worrying exception is a drop in adult borrowing that those events don't fully explain. The Board is asked to take **seven decisions**. The two that matter most are the 2026 budget and a rise in the adult fee from 20 cr to 22 cr.

## The year in numbers

| | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Loans | 101,250 | 96,480 | −4.7% |
| Members at year end | 8,412 | 8,670 | +3.1% (record) |
| Members who borrowed at least once | 74.2% | 70.6% | −3.6 pts |
| Late return rate | 10.2% | 11.7% | +1.5 pts (target 9.8%) |
| Cost per loan | 7.66 cr | 8.64 cr | +12.8% |
| Surplus / (deficit) | 4,290 cr | (4,044) cr | |
| Reserves | 126,300 cr | 122,256 cr | still above the policy minimum |

**More people are joining, fewer are borrowing, and costs are up.**

## Why loans fell

1. **Home delivery was cut back.** The coordinator retired in March and the post was left unfilled, so home delivery loans fell from 5,120 to 3,180. This is the largest single cause, and it explains most of the drop in senior and large-print borrowing.
2. **The library was open fewer hours.** The roof failed on 23 July, which led to an 8-day closure in September for a 38,500 cr repair (the Town Council paid 30,000 cr). Staff vacancies also forced the Thursday late opening to stop for 22 weeks. In total the library lost 112 opening hours.
3. **Reservation queues got longer.** Book buying was paused while the cost of the roof was uncertain. The average wait rose to 18.4 days, and to 41 days for the ten most-reserved titles.

Loans per opening hour stayed flat (38.5 against 38.7). That supports the view that the library was used as heavily as before when it was open. In November and December the year-on-year falls narrowed to about 1–2%.

## Bright spots

- Children's loans rose 10.6% to a record, and junior membership grew 7.3%.
- Online joining rose 64%, and the school sign-up scheme doubled.
- The self-service kiosk handled 45.8% of loans by the fourth quarter. It reached its third-year target in its first year, and the desk time it freed kept daytime hours open.
- Event attendance rose 16.1%.

## Concerns

- **Adult members are borrowing less.** Only 67.5% of adult members borrowed at all, so about one in three paid the fee and borrowed nothing. The report calls this the most important negative trend.
- **Late returns rose.** About 580 of the extra late returns are linked to the end of junior fines, and about 260 to a kiosk reminder fault in July and August, which has since been fixed.
- **The rest of the roof is a risk.** The other roof sections are likely to need replacing within 3–5 years, at an estimated **145,000 cr**. That is more than the library's entire reserves. It is also unresolved whether the Library or the Council is responsible for the structure under the lease.

## 2026 plan

The plan restores full staffing, home delivery and the Thursday late opening. It also adds books to shorten reservation queues, follows up with members who have stopped borrowing, and introduces text-message reminders. Targets include 99,000 loans, a 72% active-member rate and a late return rate of 10.5% or less.

## Decisions requested

1. **Budget:** spend 836,700 cr against income of 825,000 cr, with an **11,700 cr deficit** taken from reserves.
2. **Adult fee from 20 cr to 22 cr** from 1 April 2026. This would be the first rise since 2021. *Without it, reserves would end 2026 4,632 cr below the policy minimum. Management would then cut the book fund to make up the gap.*
3. **Make fine-free borrowing for juniors permanent.** Bringing fines back would recover only about 1,000 cr a year.
4. **Turn the vacant 0.6 FTE post into an Outreach and Home Delivery Assistant,** at the same grade and cost.
5. **Write off 2,784 cr of unpaid charges for lost books** (174 copies). This is already fully provided for in the accounts.
6. **Roof:** authorise the Director and Chair to negotiate responsibility with the Council and to prepare a 145,000 cr capital bid. Both would come back to the Board before anything is agreed or submitted.
7. **Approve the 2026 targets,** with a progress report at every Board meeting.

**Points the Board may want to probe:** whether the 5,968 cr headroom above the reserves minimum is enough given the roof risk, and how likely the loans target is to be met. Management itself calls it "achievable but not certain."
