# 2025 Annual Report: Board Summary

*Meeting of 19 February 2026, 10 minutes. The full report is available on request. Formal wording of the decisions is in Section 10.5 of the report. The figures are draft and unaudited; the audited accounts come to the Board in April.*

## In brief

**Seven decisions. Three need discussion, and four can be taken quickly.**

- **Discuss:** the fee rise (2) and the budget (1), which depends on it. Settle the fee first.
- **Brief:** junior fines (3) and the roof (6).
- **Take together:** the new post (4), the write-off (5) and the targets (7).

## What changed in 2025

- **Loans fell 4.7%,** to 96,480. It was the first fall in four years.
- **Membership hit a record 8,670.** Children's loans also hit a record (+10.6%).
- **Fewer members borrowed:** 70.6% borrowed at least once, down from 74.2%. Only two in three adult members borrowed at all.
- **More books came back late:** 11.7% of loans, up from 10.2%.

**Causes:** staff vacancies cut home delivery and the Thursday late opening, the roof failure closed the Library for 8 days, and a kiosk fault stopped some reminders. Management says none of these should recur. The fall in adult borrowing is the one trend they don't fully explain.

## What it cost

- **2025 ended 4,044 cr in deficit,** against the 11,500 cr deficit the Board approved. It included a 38,500 cr roof repair, of which the Town Council paid 30,000 cr.
- **The vacancies hid an underlying gap.** They saved 17,700 cr. With full staffing, 2025 would have run a deficit of about 13,000 cr even without the roof.
- **Reserves stand at 122,256 cr,** which is 21,131 cr above the policy minimum.
- **The rest of the roof** will probably need replacing within 3–5 years, at about 145,000 cr. That is more than total reserves, and it is not yet agreed whether the Library or the Council pays.
- **The Council grant is agreed only to the end of 2027.** It provides 77% of income. Its renewal, the roof and the 2027 budget all fall in the same period.

## Decisions

### Discuss

**2. Raise the adult fee from 20 cr to 22 cr** from 1 April 2026 (§10.3). It is the first rise since 2021, and the partner libraries charge 24–26 cr.
- **With the rise,** reserves end 2026 at 5,968 cr above the minimum.
- **Without it,** they end 4,632 cr below, and management would cut the book fund to close the gap.

**1. Approve the 2026 budget** (§10.2): spending of 836,700 cr, income of 825,000 cr, and a deficit of 11,700 cr paid from reserves. It restores full staffing, home delivery and the late opening.
The deficit is the same underlying gap the vacancies hid in 2025: pay is growing faster than the grant. Management says it won't propose another deficit budget, and will bring options for 2027 in October.

### Brief

**3. Make fine-free junior borrowing permanent.** It brought back 297 previously blocked junior members. It costs about 1,000 cr a year in fines not collected, plus some extra late returns.

**6. Authorise the Chair and Director to negotiate with the Town Council** over who pays for the roof, and to prepare a 145,000 cr capital bid. Nothing is committed now, and both would come back to the Board before anything is concluded or submitted.

### Take together

**4. Turn a vacant 0.6 FTE post into an Outreach and Home Delivery Assistant.** It is within budget, with no change to staff numbers.

**5. Write off 174 unpaid lost-book charges (2,784 cr).** They are already provided for, so this has no effect on the year's result.

**7. Approve the 2026 targets,** with progress reported at every Board meeting. The main targets are 99,000 loans, an active rate of 72%, and a late return rate of 10.5% or less. The loans target is based on November–December 2025, when loans were only 1–2% below 2024.

## Questions for management, ideally answered before the meeting

1. **Fee income (decisions 1–2).** The budget charges 22 cr to all 5,300 adult members, but anyone who renews in January–March pays 20 cr until 2027. If renewals are spread evenly through the year, that is about 2,650 cr less income, which would cut the headroom to about 3,300 cr. Is the income estimate right?
2. **Fines (decision 5).** 580 cr of fines outstanding at the end of 2024 doesn't appear in the 2025 figures as collected, waived or written off. What happened to it?
