# Wrenmere Lending Library: 2025 Annual Report, Board Summary

*For the Board meeting of 19 February 2026. The report says all its figures are synthetic.*

## The year in one paragraph

More people joined the Library in 2025, but they borrowed less. Loans fell 4.7% (101,250 → 96,480), the first fall in four years. Membership reached a record 8,670 (+258), and children's loans reached a record 25,610 (+10.6%). Most of the fall in loans came from three events that won't recur: a roof failure that closed the Library for 8 days, two long staff vacancies that cut home delivery and the Thursday late opening, and a fault with the new kiosk that stopped some reminder emails. The worrying trend is that fewer adults are borrowing, and the year's events don't fully explain it.

## What drove the results

- **Home delivery was cut** after the coordinator retired and the post was left vacant. Home delivery loans fell from 5,120 to 3,180. This was the biggest single cause of the drop in loans, and it hit large print (−13.9%) and senior members (−16.3%) hardest.
- **Opening hours fell by 112:** the 8-day roof closure, plus 22 weeks without the Thursday late opening. Loans per opening hour stayed flat (38.5 vs 38.7).
- **Reservation queues got longer** (18.4 days on average, 41 days for the ten most-reserved titles) because book buying was paused while the roof cost was unknown.
- **Fewer adults are borrowing.** Only 67.5% of adult members borrowed at all, so about one in three paid the fee without borrowing a book. The report calls this the most important adverse trend.
- **The kiosk worked well.** It reached a 45.8% share of loans in Q4, against a business-case target of 45% by year three, and it freed the desk time that kept daytime hours open.
- **Late returns rose** for three reasons: the end of junior fines (about 580 extra late returns, mostly less than a week late), the kiosk reminder fault (about 260 extra), and suspended phone follow-ups during the vacancies.

## Risks for the Board

- **The rest of the roof** is likely to need replacing within 3–5 years, at about 145,000 cr. That is more than the Library's total reserves. Who pays for it under the lease is still unresolved.
- **Reserve headroom will be thin.** With the fee increase, reserves are projected at only 5,968 cr above the minimum at the end of 2026. Without it, they fall 4,632 cr below.
- **The 2027 budget** will need balancing options, which management will bring in October 2026. Management says it won't propose another deficit budget.

## The 2026 plan

The plan restores full staffing, home delivery and the late opening. It also:
- buys one copy for every five outstanding reservations
- follows up lapsed and online joiners
- adds text-message reminders
- commissions a survey of the remaining roof sections

The main targets are 99,000 loans, an active rate of 72%, a late return rate of 10.5% or less, and reservation waits of 15 days or less.

## Seven decisions requested

1. **Approve the 2026 budget:** 836,700 cr spending, 825,000 cr income, and an 11,700 cr deficit paid from reserves.
2. **Raise the adult fee from 20 cr to 22 cr** from 1 April 2026. It is the first rise since 2021 and would still be below the two partner libraries (24 and 26 cr). If the Board says no, management would cut about 4,632 cr from the book fund.
3. **Make fine-free junior borrowing permanent.** It has brought back 297 previously blocked junior members, and reinstating fines would recover only about 1,000 cr a year.
4. **Turn the vacant 0.6 FTE post into an Outreach and Home Delivery Assistant,** at no extra cost.
5. **Write off 174 unpaid replacement charges (2,784 cr).** They are already fully provided for, so this doesn't affect the year's result.
6. **Authorise the Director and Chair to negotiate with the Town Council** over roof responsibility and to prepare a 145,000 cr capital bid. Both would come back to the Board before anything is concluded or submitted.
7. **Approve the 2026 targets,** with a progress report at every Board meeting.

## Bottom line

The fall in loans has clear causes, and most of them can be fixed. The budget depends on the fee increase to keep reserves above the minimum. The two biggest long-term issues are the roof and the fall in adult borrowing.
