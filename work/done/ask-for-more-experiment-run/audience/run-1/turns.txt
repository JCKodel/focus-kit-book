# Wrenmere Lending Library: 2025 annual report, board summary

**For the Board meeting of 19 February 2026**

## The year in one paragraph

2025 was a hard year, and most of the reasons are known and temporary. Membership reached a record **8,670** (+3.1%), and children's borrowing reached a record **25,610** loans (+10.6%). But total loans fell **4.7% to 96,480**, the first fall in four years. Fewer members actually borrowed (**70.6%**, down from 74.2%), and more books came back late (**11.7%**, up from 10.2%). Three things caused most of this: the July roof failure, which closed the Library for 8 days; two staff vacancies of nine months each, which cut home delivery and Thursday late opening; and a kiosk software fault that stopped due-date reminders for about six weeks. The Library ran a **4,044 cr deficit**, which was met from reserves. Without the one-off roof repair it would have had a surplus of 4,456 cr.

## Key figures

| | 2024 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Loans | 101,250 | 96,480 | −4.7% |
| Members at year end | 8,412 | 8,670 | +3.1% |
| Members who borrowed at least once | 74.2% | 70.6% | −3.6 pts |
| Late return rate | 10.2% | 11.7% | +1.5 pts |
| Expenditure (cr) | 775,610 | 833,700 | +7.5% |
| Income (cr) | 779,900 | 829,656 | +6.4% |
| Reserves (cr) | 126,300 | 122,256 | above the policy minimum of 101,125 |

## Why loans fell

1. **Home delivery was cut** because its coordinator post was left vacant. That meant about 1,940 fewer loans, and large-print loans fell 13.9%. Excluding home delivery, loans to senior members fell only 6%.
2. **112 fewer opening hours** (the September closure and 22 weeks without Thursday evenings) cost an estimated 1,500–2,000 loans. Loans per opening hour stayed flat at 38.5.
3. **Reservation waits got longer**, from 16.9 to 18.4 days on average and 41 days for the top ten titles. Book buying was paused from April to August while the roof cost was uncertain.
4. **Adult borrowing fell in a way the events above don't explain.** Management sees this as the most worrying trend: about one adult member in three paid the fee but borrowed nothing.

Since November, with full staff back and late opening restored, loans have run only 1–2% below 2024.

## What went well

- **Membership:** 1,236 people joined, a record. Online joining rose 64% and the school sign-up scheme doubled.
- **Children:** junior membership rose 7.3%. Ending junior fines unblocked 412 cards, and 297 of those children borrowed again. The summer reading challenge enrolled 612 children and 418 finished it.
- **Kiosk:** it handled 31% of loans in its first year and 45.8% in Q4, which met the third-year target early. The desk time it freed is what kept daytime hours open during the vacancies.
- **Events:** attendance rose 16.1%.

## Risks the Board should note

- **The rest of the roof** is the same age as the failed section and is likely to need replacing within 3–5 years, at an estimated **145,000 cr**, more than the Library's entire reserves. Who pays for the structure under the lease with the Town Council is unresolved.
- **Reserves headroom is thin.** Under the 2026 budget only about 6,000 cr would remain above the minimum, and that depends on the fee increase.
- **Ending junior fines has a cost:** about 580 extra late returns, mostly less than a week late. The income lost is about 1,000 cr a year.

## 2026 plan

The plan restores full staffing, home delivery and Thursday late opening. It also adds more copies of heavily reserved titles (one copy per five reservations), follows up lapsed and new online members, introduces text-message reminders, and commissions a survey of the roof's condition.

Main targets for 2026:
- 99,000 loans
- 72% of members borrowing
- a late return rate of 10.5% or less
- reservation waits of 15 days or less

The budget is **836,700 cr spending and 825,000 cr income**, a planned deficit of **11,700 cr** from reserves. Management says it will not propose another deficit budget for 2027.

## Seven decisions requested

1. **Approve the 2026 budget** with an 11,700 cr deficit.
2. **Raise the adult fee from 20 cr to 22 cr** from 1 April 2026, the first rise since 2021. *Without it, reserves would fall about 4,632 cr below the minimum, and management would cut the book fund to make up the gap.*
3. **Make fine-free borrowing for children permanent.**
4. **Turn the vacant 0.6 FTE post into an Outreach and Home Delivery Assistant** at the same cost, and authorise recruitment.
5. **Write off 174 unpaid replacement charges totalling 2,784 cr.** These are already fully provided for, so the write-off has no effect on the year's result.
6. **Authorise talks with the Town Council** on who is responsible for the roof, and the preparation of a bid for about 145,000 cr in capital funding. Both would come back to the Board before anything is concluded or submitted.
7. **Approve the 2026 targets**, with progress reported at every Board meeting.
