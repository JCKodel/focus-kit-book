## Notes

- Dell'Acqua et al.: cite the published version (Organization Science 2026, DOI 10.1287/orsc.2025.21838) for 12.2%, 25.1%, 29.9% to 33.9% and 19 percentage points. The 43% against 17% split exists only in the 2023 working paper. The 2026 article also gives -24.5% against -13.9% for the two AI arms outside the frontier, where the 2023 paper said 24 and 13 percentage points.
- Skitka et al. 1999: the open author's copy (row 1) gives exact pages. Use "59% against 97% accuracy on the six events the aid did not flag" (41% against 3% missed) and "on average 65% of the wrong recommendations followed". Do not write "65% of participants".
- Randazzo et al.: cite HBS Working Paper 26-036 rather than the institute summary. The paper's N add up to 243, not 244, and 63/244 is 25.8% although the paper prints 27%. Use the paper's own figures with their N.
- Shen and Tamkin: the paper's numbers (17%, 4.15 of 27 points) differ in form from the blog's (50% against 67%). Pick one source and say which. Do not call the sample "mostly junior" from the paper.
- Cui et al. and Lou and Sun are now peer reviewed (Management Science 2026; Journal of Computational Social Science 2025). Kosmyna et al. is still a preprint, and the Stankovic critique is itself an arXiv comment.
- METR: no result from the redesigned study has been published. The 2026 figures come from a study METR itself calls biased by selection.
- Mosier and Skitka 1996 p. 205 can only be cited second-hand ("quoted in Mosier and Manzey 2019") unless someone opens the book.
- The "45% against 5%" snippet now has a source (Cheung, Maier, Lieder, PNAS 2025). It measures action against omission framing in moral dilemmas, not a rate of changing answers.
