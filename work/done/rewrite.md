# rewrite

**Objective.** A developer or a team that reads the book understands the engineering practices the method rests on, learns focus-kit as a process the whole team runs, and sees in every chapter what the team or the project gains, in a book short enough to be read to the end.

**Behaviour.**

* The book has three movements, in this order: the base (a shared language, with evidence), the method (how to do it, didactically), and the team (git, tools, adoption, cost), and every chapter proves a gain for the team or the project, with a number or a sourced claim, in a section of its own.
* No chapter narrates a run: no brief, no transcript of the agent's turns, no diff of a page, no `git status`, no headless flags, no commit hash as evidence. A real number or a real artifact appears where it proves a point, and the text says where it comes from.
* The base teaches, before the process, what every reader must share: why process when AI writes fast, how an agent sees a project, Spec-Driven Development, simplicity (KISS, YAGNI, DRY), rules as pure functions and exceptions as values, features instead of layers with dependencies only where a fake exists, the four pieces of FOCUS, and a test for each piece. Each names the practice, who named it and where, the problem it answers, and what the team gains.
* The method is taught from the reader's problem to the practice to its use, with focus-kit's own files as the reference: the documents, the four commands, the queue, the page, verify and proof, the milestone review, the governor, and the project as the team's assistant.
* A reader who is not a developer (a manager, an analyst, a designer) can follow the prologue, Part I's first three chapters, Part II and Part IV, and sees where the process answers their questions: what is pending, how it is going, who owes an answer.
* Evidence comes from Ninjobs (named, its process artifacts and counted numbers), Case A and Case B (anonymous, with the author's approval of every passage, docs/00 OD-3), the published literature, and this book's own repository where it is the example of a project that is not software. No guided project, no brownfield project, no exercises.
* Case A ran on the process (a client project on a low-code platform, 73 deliveries done in 8 commit days) and is the book's team case. Case B did not run on the process: it is a consultancy's proposal for a client's adoption program, with an internal note that maps the client's problems to the method and an analysis whose every number carried its provenance; its findings had no queue to carry them, and one was lost in the next version. No chapter implies that Case B ran on focus-kit.
* Code examples are TypeScript, written for the chapter, short, complete enough to compile in the reader's head, and the book says once that they are written for it. Every number and every quoted artifact (a page, a queue line, a rules file) stays real and sourced; ADR-0017 records the split, and docs/00 §Values and AGENTS.md say it the same way.

**Contract.**

*The new contents.* Chapter files are renamed and renumbered in both editions; a chapter that exists today is rewritten from its plan below, reusing what the audit marked as worth keeping. Chapter shape (docs/04): title, opening (at most three sentences), sections, `## What the team gains` (pt: `## O que o time ganha`), `## Key points` (at most five). No exercises.

**What happened.**

* The author's decisions, by interactive questions in the `/propose` conversation: rewrite everything in one session; evidence only Ninjobs, Case A, Case B and the literature; the base with all four blocks, the four pieces included, before the method; abandon the guided project; write the unwritten chapters now; the project as assistant promised in the prologue and taught right after the method; no exercises; the private folders of Case A and Case B read by the agent and paraphrased for the author's approval.
* What diverged from the first plan: Case B did not run on the process. Its folder holds three versions of a proposal, a deck, an internal note that maps a client's problems to the method, and an analysis with a provenance mark on every number, and one commit. The plan's chapters 22 and 24 were rewritten to use it as the shape of a pitch and as the contrast of findings that had no queue to carry them; docs/00 says so. The planned chapter "the commit as the human review" became a section of chapter 15, and "customizing" merged into chapter 21 with the team's tools, so the book has 25 chapters, the prologue included.
* Research, by three agents, anonymized at the source: the Case A and Case B survey, the Ninjobs story and counts with their commands, and a bibliography of primary sources. Their reports and the style contract lived in the session's scratchpad, outside the repository.
* Three chapters (00, 05, 14) were written first as the voice models; nine agents wrote the other 22 in English from the plan, the models and the research; seven agents translated. Every chapter passed the prose and em dash checks on its own before the whole was verified.
* Numbers fixed once: Ninjobs's 37,228 spec lines keep the book's own `wc -l` count with its ADR's rounded figure named in the note; 91 deliveries at the opening (its ADR-0026) against 93 finished pages to 2026-09-23; tokens per delivery over 93 pages, cache reads counted apart.
* Cut: the book's own artifacts as examples (its AGENTS.md, its queue diffs, its verify slot), the three tools' run beyond three counts, every clinic and CLAHub excerpt, every headless detail, every commit hash in the text. The run records in `work/done/*-run/` stay, and two notes point to them (the three tools' run in chapter 3; the parallel run in chapter 20).
* Length: 39,550 words in English against 50,222 before, with 25 chapters against 19, and far less code; the longest are chapters 19 (2,631 words), 12 (2,186), 20 (2,059) and 4 (2,043). No length target was set (ADR-0015); the author decides after reading whether any of them cuts further.
